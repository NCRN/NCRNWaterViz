Introduction

The National Park Service preserves unimpaired the natural and cultural resources and values of the National Park System. The National Captial Region Inventory and Monitoring Network (NCRN) is part of the National Park Service Inventory and Monitoring Division, which conducts science in National Parks. NCRN conducts science, like water quality monitoring, in the National Park Service's National Capital Region, which includes parts of Virginia, West Virginia, Maryland, and the District of Columbia, USA.

Methods

NCRN's water quality methods are available here. The citation for NCRN's methods is in the Citations & References tab above.

Sampling locations

  • As of January 2025, NCRN monitors water quality at 37 sites in ten National Parks in the National Capital Region
  • Each NCRN water quality site is a perennial, non-tidal, wadeable, 1st - 4th order freshwater stream with at least one kilometer of its reach within the property boundaries of a National Capital Region National Park

    • Sampling frequency

  • NCRN began monitoring water quality in 2005 and monitoring is ongoing
  • From 2005 through 2018, NCRN generally visited each water quality monitoring site once per month
  • Since 2018, NCRN has generally visited each water quality monitoring site every-other-month

    • Water Quality Parameters (active as of 2024-12-31)

  • Water temperature
  • Air pressure (i.e. barometric pressure)
  • Dissolved Oxygen concentration (DO concentration mg/L)
  • Dissolved Oxygen saturation (DO saturation %)
  • Specific Conductance
  • Conductivity
  • Salinity
  • pH
  • Air Pressure (i.e., barometric pressure)

    • Laboratory Parameters (active as of 2024-12-31)

  • Acid Neutralizing Capacity (ANC)
  • Total Nitrogen concentration (TN)
  • Total Phosphorus concentration (TP)

    • Water Quantity Parameters (active as of 2024-12-31)

  • Discharge
  • Wetted width (stream width across stream crossection where discharge was measured)
  • Stream Velocity (average water velocity across stream crossection where discharge was measured)
  • Depth (average water depth across stream crossection where discharge was measured)

    • Water Quality Parameters (inactive as of 2024 and excluded from this webapp)

  • Total Dissolved Nitrogen concentration (retired 2018)
  • Total Dissolved Phosphorus concentration (retired 2018)

    • Data quality

    NCRN estimates this dataset to be "High-Quality" because it has passed internal and external review and is subject to an ongoing quality control process.

    1. Completeness
  • NCRN's active sites and parameters are included in this website
  • Retired monitoring sites were excluded from this website. NCRN retires sites when they stop fitting our protocol, such as when a stream that used to flow year-round becomes seasonally dry.
  • Retired monitoring parameters were excluded from this website. NCRN retires parameters as required by operational constraints. For example, NCRN stopped measuring Total Dissolved Nitrogen concentration in 2018 and immediately began collecting Total Nitrogen concentration instead.
  • Categorical and text parameters, like descriptions of stream stage and epilithic algae, were excluded from this website
  • NCRN's full water quality dataset, including retired sites and parameters, is available for download from NPS DataStore
    • 2. Accuracy
  • NCRN calibrates field instruments (e.g., YSI ProDSS handheld) to manufacturer specifications before each site visit
  • A certified, third-party laboratory (UMCES Appalachian Lab) measures NCRN's water chemistry results (ANC, TP, TN)
    • 3. Consistency and validity
  • NCRN's data governance procedures ensure measurements, parameters, units, and location attributes are consistent through time and among-and-within sites
    • 4. Uniqueness
  • NCRN found no duplicated records in the dataset
